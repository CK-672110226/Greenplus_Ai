# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**User / Seller** — CMU students in dormitories and digital nomads/expats in Tambon Suthep. They sort recyclable waste at home or in their dorm, want to know what it's worth before making the trip, and need to find the nearest shop that will actually accept what they have. Mobile-first, often outdoors, scanning mid-task.

**Buyer / Shop** — Local scrap-shop operators. They run a physical counter, toggle what they accept each day, and need to see incoming bookings and marketplace demand at a glance. Speed and clarity matter more than elegance. They may be less technically fluent than the User role.

**Admin** — Platform moderator. Approves new shops, monitors heatmaps, handles flagged marketplace posts. Low-frequency, high-stakes actions in a sparse dashboard.

**Shared context:** bilingual Thai/English audience in a tight geographic pilot zone (CMU rear zone + Tambon Suthep). Thai is the primary language; English is a peer — not a fallback or an afterthought.

## Product Purpose

GreenPlus Ai is a waste-to-value platform: it connects people who have recyclable waste with shops that buy it, using AI scanning and real-time Chiang Mai market rates to give an honest valuation before anyone leaves the house. Success means a User walks to the right shop, gets a fair price, and the shop fills its intake queue without chasing supply.

## Brand Personality

Honest · Practical · Community

The voice is direct and useful: a number, a grade, a route. Not motivational copy. Not leaf emoji. The platform believes recycling is already worth doing for financial and community reasons — it doesn't need to manipulate users into it with streaks or badges.

## Anti-references

**Gamified eco-app** (Duolingo-for-sustainability aesthetic): no streak flames, no confetti on every action, no "you helped save X trees" banners. Eco-Points exist as a lightweight record, not a dopamine loop.

**Generic green SaaS**: no gradient hero sections, no stock photos of smiling people holding recycled bottles, no leaf/globe/earth icon sets, no sustainability-report color palettes (teal + white + UN blue). The app serves a scrap market, not a CSR department.

## Design Principles

1. **Data is the UI.** A price, a grade, a distance — these are the product. Decoration that doesn't carry information should be absent. If an element can't answer "what does the user need to know or do?", remove it.

2. **Function earns trust.** The neo-brutalist visual language (flat shadows, ink borders, paper background, monospace data type) signals that nothing is hidden behind gloss. Buyers and sellers both need to trust that what they see is what they get.

3. **Community-first copy.** Write for someone at a student dorm sorting bottles, not a sustainability consultant. Short sentences. Local context. Thai-first phrasing that doesn't sound translated.

4. **Honest about complexity.** Recycling has real rules: grades, contamination, material types. The app doesn't simplify these into a false ease. Rule cards, grade labels, and waste-handling guidance are first-class UI, not fine print.

5. **Bilingual by design.** Thai and English are peers. Layout, spacing, and font choices must work for both scripts simultaneously. Thai text is not an afterthought squeezed into an English-shaped box.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Minimum 44px tap targets throughout (mobile-primary). Contrast ratios meet AA for both Thai and Latin text at all sizes. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any animation. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning (grades use both color and label).
